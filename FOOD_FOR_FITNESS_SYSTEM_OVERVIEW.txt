FOODFORFITNESS
SYSTEM OVERVIEW, FEATURES, AND CURRENT SYSTEM ANALYSIS

Prepared from the application source code and project documentation
Review date: October 15, 2026

======================================================================
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
======================================================================

FoodForFitness is a Filipino-focused nutrition and wellness tracking
application. It brings together food logging, estimated nutrition
tracking, profile-based daily targets, health readings, reminders, and
AI-assisted food and nutrition guidance in one mobile-friendly system.

The application is designed for people who want a convenient way to
understand their food intake and wellness habits, especially in relation
to Filipino dishes and common health goals or conditions. It is a
tracking and educational support tool. It is not a medical device, a
diagnostic system, or a replacement for advice from a doctor or
registered dietitian.

The user interface is built with React and Tailwind CSS. The project
supports a browser/PWA experience and an Android app shell through
Capacitor. Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore integrations are
present in the source code, but whether a live Firebase project is
configured depends on the runtime environment and credentials.

======================================================================
2. SYSTEM PURPOSE AND PROBLEM IT ADDRESSES
======================================================================

FoodForFitness aims to help users:

- Record meals and serving sizes.
- See estimated calories, macronutrients, and selected micronutrients.
- Compare food intake with daily targets.
- Browse Filipino foods and receive profile-aware food suggestions.
- Record selected body and health readings.
- Set meal, hydration, medication, or health-check reminders.
- Ask NutriBot general nutrition questions by typing or, when supported,
  using speech.
- Estimate the nutrition content of a meal photo with AI.

The main idea is to make nutrition tracking more approachable and
culturally relevant by centering Filipino meals, using a simple
dashboard, and offering multiple ways to record food.

======================================================================
3. OVERALL THEME AND USER EXPERIENCE
======================================================================

The system uses a friendly, supportive, health-oriented visual theme:

- Teal and emerald colors communicate wellness, nutrition, and progress.
- Filipino identity is reflected in the FoodForFitness name, the
  "Mabuhay" greeting, and the Filipino food catalog.
- The navigation is designed for small screens, with a bottom bar for
  Dashboard, Food Log, Scanner, Health, and AI Bot.
- Cards, progress bars, status colors, and charts present information
  quickly instead of requiring users to read dense reports.
- A light/dark theme can be selected. The preference is stored in the
  browser's local storage.
- The design aims to feel like a practical daily companion, not a
  hospital record system.

The tone of the AI assistant is intended to be encouraging and concise,
with Taglish or English responses. Because the app stores health-related
profile details and gives nutrition estimates, the interface and
documentation should continue to make clear that outputs are
informational and may be inaccurate.

4. FUNCTIONS CURRENTLY PRESENT IN THE APP
======================================================================

4.1 Account access and profile

- Email/password registration and sign-in are implemented through
  Firebase Authentication when Firebase is configured.
- Google sign-in is also implemented.
- A Demo Sign In option is shown when Firebase is not configured.
- New account registration asks for a name, gender option, email,
  password, and acceptance of the Terms and Conditions plus
  acknowledgement of the Privacy Policy.
- New account profiles record the terms and privacy versions and
  acceptance/acknowledgement timestamps.
- Profile setup collects name, gender, age, weight, height, activity
  level, weight goal, and selected health conditions.
- The profile view allows users to edit setup information, sign out,
  inspect/download app data as JSON, and clear app local-storage data
  from the current device.
- Signed-in users can request deletion of the Firebase Auth account and
  the app's known Firestore profile and food-log records. The flow
  requires re-authentication and confirmation.
- A Privacy Policy dialog describes app storage, AI processing,
  provider limitations, and available data controls.
- The available setup condition choices currently include diabetes,
  hypertension, gout, chronic kidney disease (CKD), PCOS, and
  dyslipidemia.

4.2 Dashboard and nutrition progress

- Displays calorie intake, calorie target, and protein, carbohydrate,
  and fat totals with progress indicators.
- Displays sugar and sodium limits and warnings when totals exceed the
  configured targets.
- Displays catalog-provided micronutrient totals, including fiber, calcium,
  iron, potassium, and selected vitamins; unavailable values are not guessed.
- Uses profile information to calculate approximate calorie needs and
  condition-related nutrition limits.

4.3 Filipino food log

- Includes a bundled Filipino food catalog that loads when the Food Log
  page is opened.
- Supports searching by food name or category.
- Supports browsing by Go, Grow, Glow, and Other food groups.
- Provides profile-aware suggestions using simple scoring rules. For
  example, some foods are scored based on reported diabetes,
  hypertension, or gout flags.
- Lets users set a serving quantity and add food to their log.
- Scales catalog nutrient values by serving quantity and marks unavailable
  values as unknown rather than substituting category-based estimates.
- Lets users remove food-log entries.
- Provides period-based calorie and nutrient summaries for analytics.

4.4 Camera and photo food analysis

- Supports taking a photo with the device camera or selecting an image
  from the photo library.
- Resizes/compresses the image before sending it for analysis.
- Uses the Gemini multimodal API to identify up to three foods and
  estimate their nutrition, portion multiplier, allergens, purine
  level, confidence, and brief health/portion notes.
- Displays the result for review and can add recognized items to the
  food log.
- Presents warning indicators for selected high-sugar, high-sodium, or
  high-purine results based on profile settings.
- The scanner is focused on meal/plate photo analysis through camera
  capture or image upload. Barcode scanning and dedicated nutrition
  facts label reading are not included.

4.5 Vitals and nutrition analytics

- Allows users to enter weight, blood pressure, and glucose readings.
- Displays BMI, recent readings, condition-related nutrition insights,
  and food-log trends for 7-day or 30-day periods.
- Provides averages, goal-day counts, nutrient totals, and a calorie
  trend visualization based on logged food dates.
- The current vital readings are held in application state and are not
  durably saved to Firestore or local storage.
- Initial health readings are sample data in the source code and should
  not be mistaken for real user readings.

4.6 Reminders and notifications

- Supports adding, editing, and removing reminders.
- Reminder types include meal, hydration, medication, and health check.
- Supports daily, weekday, and one-time repeat settings.
- Stores reminders in local storage and, for configured signed-in users,
  in the Firestore profile document.
- Uses Capacitor Local Notifications for Android.
- Uses browser notifications for web/PWA where supported.
- Reminder scheduling depends on device/browser permissions. Web
  reminders require the app's JavaScript to be running for the
  in-app checking behavior; there is no server-side push scheduler.

4.7 NutriBot and accessibility-oriented controls

- Provides an AI nutrition chat using the user's profile context,
  selected conditions, BMI, goals, and configured nutrition targets.
- The current deployed chatbot uses Gemini. A local integration point
  is being prepared for a custom fine-tuned nutrition model to answer
  first, with Gemini as fallback; the trained model is not yet connected
  to the deployed app.
- Stores conversation history in local storage, keyed by profile name.
- Provides speech-to-text where the runtime exposes the browser speech
  recognition API.
- Provides text-to-speech using a native Capacitor plugin on Android
  and browser speech synthesis on web where available.
- Speech recognition support varies by browser and device.

4.8 PWA and Android delivery

- The web project includes a PWA manifest and a service worker for
  app-shell and runtime caching.
- The manifest configures standalone display and a portrait-oriented
  experience.
- Capacitor wraps the built web app for Android and routes selected
  platform capabilities such as camera, speech, and notifications.
- The install/download prompt is intended for the web experience and
  is hidden in the native Capacitor app.

======================================================================
5. HIGH-LEVEL SYSTEM FLOW
======================================================================

1. The app starts and checks for an existing Firebase Auth session.
2. If the user is not authenticated or the profile is not set up, the
   app shows the account/profile screen.
3. A Firebase user can register or sign in if Firebase configuration
   is available. Demo Sign In is available when Firebase is absent.
4. The user enters profile details and preferences.
5. The shared application hook manages profile, food-log, reminder,
   vital, and navigation state.
6. Food entries are selected from the catalog or created from a photo
   analysis.
7. The app calculates totals and compares them with profile-based
   targets.
8. The dashboard, health analytics, and NutriBot use this state to
   present progress or guidance.
9. Depending on platform, data is held in memory/local storage,
   Firebase Authentication/Firestore, or sent to an external AI
   provider for processing.

======================================================================
6. IMPLEMENTATION AND DATA STORAGE
======================================================================

Frontend:
- React 19 and JavaScript/JSX
- Vite build and development tooling
- Tailwind CSS styling

Platform:
- Capacitor 8 Android shell
- Web/PWA manifest and service worker
- Platform adapters choose web or native implementations for selected
  features

Cloud services:
- Firebase Authentication for account identity
- Cloud Firestore for profile, reminders, and signed-in food logs
- Gemini for food image analysis and chat fallback
- Custom fine-tuned model for primary chat responses (prototype in progress)

Current storage behavior:

- Profile and reminders are saved to a Firestore profile document when
  Firebase is configured and the user is signed in.
- Signed-in food logs are stored under the user's Firestore foodLogs
  subcollection.
- Guest/demo food logs are held in React memory and are not durable
  after a page reload or app reset.
- Vital readings are currently held in React memory only.
- NutriBot history, theme preference, reminder cache, and a food catalog
  cache use local storage.
- The food catalog itself is bundled application data, not a Firestore
  collection.
- No Firebase Storage use was found in the implementation.

======================================================================
7. SYSTEM ANALYSIS: STRENGTHS
======================================================================

- A coherent set of nutrition, health, reminder, and AI features is
  available in one mobile-oriented interface.
- Filipino food is central to both food discovery and the scanner/chat
  prompts, which differentiates the system from generic calorie logs.
- The profile informs nutrition targets and some food suggestions,
  rather than presenting exactly the same experience to every user.
- Camera and photo upload give users alternative ways to record meals.
- The application separates major responsibilities into feature
  components, a shared state hook, service modules, and platform
  adapters.
- The app has both web and native delivery paths, with platform-aware
  notification and speech/text-to-speech support.
- User-entered food logs can be persisted to Firestore for configured
  Firebase accounts.
- The current app includes a medical-information disclaimer in the
  Terms and Conditions and Privacy Policy content.

8. KNOWN LIMITATIONS AND AREAS TO IMPROVE
======================================================================

8.1 Nutrition accuracy and clinical safety

- Food catalog values and
  AI-derived meal values are estimates. Actual recipes, ingredients,
  and serving sizes vary.
- AI confidence and nutrient results should not be treated as verified
  laboratory or medical data.
- Target rules are simplified application calculations, not a
  clinically validated personalized nutrition prescription.
- The application should avoid presenting condition-based suggestions
  as diagnosis or treatment.
- Allergen and dietary suitability estimates need user verification.

8.2 Dashboard date scope

- Food-log totals are currently calculated from the full loaded log.
- The Dashboard labels these as today's nutrition, but signed-in logs
  can include older entries. This can make the dashboard's "today"
  totals include more than today's food.
- The analytics page groups entries by date, but the main dashboard
  should also filter by the current local date.

8.3 Data persistence and demo mode

- Demo Sign In is a temporary exploration path, not a durable account.
- Guest/demo food logs and vitals are not durably saved.
- Sample vital readings are preloaded in source code and should be
  removed or clearly labeled before production use.
- Clearing app/browser data removes local-only information and can
  reset the app to its initial state.
- There is no automatic migration of guest food logs into a Firebase
  account.

8.4 Authentication and terms consent

- Firebase integrations require the correct runtime configuration and
  enabled Firebase providers.
- The Google sign-in popup runs before consent is checked for a new app
  profile. The app profile is not written until consent is recorded,
  but Firebase Authentication may create the identity first.
- Terms/privacy version and timestamp fields are recorded for new
  profiles. Existing accounts are not automatically asked to consent
  again when documents change.
- The policy text is an implementation-level notice, not legal advice.
  The project owner still needs to confirm the official privacy contact,
  review the legal language, and ensure the described process and
  retention practices match the deployed system.

8.5 AI and privacy

- Chat context, including selected profile and health information, is
  sent to an AI provider for answers. Food images are sent to Gemini for
  analysis.
- When the custom trained-model endpoint is configured, chat messages
  are sent there first; requests that need fallback or cannot reach the
  endpoint are sent to Gemini. This model integration is a prototype
  and is not yet connected to the deployed app.
- Users should be clearly informed of this processing and should avoid
  entering sensitive details that are not needed.
- Any API key bundled into a browser/mobile frontend can be extracted
  by a user. Production AI calls should be reviewed for a secure
  backend/proxy design, rate limiting, and provider-key protection.
- Users can view/download the data currently available in the app and
  clear local-storage data on the current device.
- Account deletion attempts to remove the user's Firestore profile and
  food-log documents and Firebase Authentication account. It cannot
  remove AI-provider copies, data on other devices, or external
  platform backups/logs. Firebase security rules and deletion behavior
  must be tested in the deployed project.
- The project still needs a verified privacy contact channel and an
  owner-approved retention and legal review before public release.

8.6 Feature completeness and testing

- Barcode scanning and dedicated nutrition facts label reading are not
  included; the scanner currently handles meal photo capture/upload.
- Browser speech recognition and notifications vary by browser,
  operating system, permission, and network availability.
- Reminder delivery on web is not guaranteed when the app is closed;
  the app does not use a server-side push service.
- Firestore security rules, deployed database configuration, and
  production hosting cannot be verified from the application source
  alone.
- The application should be tested on target Android devices and
  browsers, including permission denial, offline behavior, data reset,
  and account transitions.

======================================================================
9. RECOMMENDED PRIORITIES
======================================================================

Before presenting a production-ready system:

1. Correct dashboard food totals to show only the selected/current day.
2. Decide which data must persist: especially vitals and guest/demo
   food logs, or label their current temporary behavior clearly.
3. Remove sample vital values from real-user views or mark them
   explicitly as sample data.
4. Have the project owner/adviser review the Privacy Policy and Terms,
   add a verified privacy contact channel, and define retention and
   material-change notice procedures.
5. Review AI data handling and move provider secrets behind a secure
   server-side endpoint before public release.
6. Add backend validation and confirm Firestore security rules prevent
   users from reading or modifying another user's data.
7. Keep the scanner focused on camera capture and photo upload; barcode
   and nutrition-label scanning are intentionally out of current scope.
8. Add tests for calculations, date filtering, persistence, consent,
   notifications, and account flows.
9. Obtain adviser/qualified professional review of nutrition limits,
   health claims, legal language, and privacy practices.

======================================================================
10. OVERALL CONCLUSION
======================================================================

FoodForFitness is a mobile-first Filipino nutrition and wellness
tracking system with a strong feature concept: combine familiar local
foods, personal progress tracking, reminders, camera-based meal
estimation, and an AI nutrition assistant.

The current implementation is a functional prototype with meaningful
web and Android foundations. Its strongest value is the integration of
these user-facing tools in a culturally relevant interface. Its main
risks are the approximate nature of nutrition/AI output, uneven data
persistence, an identified mismatch between dashboard labels and
food-log date aggregation, and privacy/production safeguards that still
need deployment-specific verification and owner review.

For a capstone presentation, distinguish clearly between:
- Implemented and testable features;
- Features that require Firebase/API configuration or device
  permissions;
- Demo-only or estimate-based behavior; and
- Recommended future work.

The system should be described as an educational wellness and tracking
application, not as a medical diagnosis or treatment product.
